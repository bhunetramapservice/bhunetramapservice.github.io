Telangana survey number map: how to read it with the pahani and ROR-1B

BHU-NETRA Map Service · 09-11-2026

In Telangana, agricultural land is identified by the district, the mandal, the revenue village and the survey number. The village map shows where each survey number lies, while the written records, mainly the pahani and the Record of Rights (ROR-1B), say who holds it, how large it is and what kind of land it is. This guide explains how the map and the records fit together and what is needed when the survey numbers must be seen on a satellite image or used in a GIS or CAD file.

1. Where the map and the record come from

Telangana land records are kept by the state Revenue Department. Revenue work runs through the district, the mandal (headed by the Tahsildar) and the revenue village. The revenue office is the right place to start for any survey number, and its record is what counts in a transaction.

2. The terms you will meet

  • Revenue village: the unit for which one village map is drawn; it can differ from the gram panchayat.
  • Survey number: the number of one parcel on the village map, the basic unit of the land record.
  • Sub-division: a later split of a survey number, written with letters or numbers such as 123/A or 123/1/2.
  • Pahani (adangal): the village account of each survey number with its area, land type, holder and cultivation.
  • ROR-1B: the Record of Rights that lists the land held by each pattadar with survey numbers and extent.
  • Pattadar passbook: the holder's book that repeats the land shown against the khata in the record.
  • Tippan / FMB: the field measurement sketch of one survey number with its side lengths.

3. Reading the village map

The village map shows survey number boundaries with the number written inside each parcel, together with roads, tanks, streams, canals and the village boundary. Survey numbers usually run as one series across the village. Many have been split since the original survey, and the sub-divisions are often not drawn on the old village map at all; their shape then comes from the tippan of that survey number or from a fresh measurement. A large survey number on the map may therefore stand for several holdings on the ground.

4. Linking the map to the pahani and ROR-1B

Take the survey number from the map, choose the same district, mandal and village in the land record, and search by survey number. The record gives the extent, the land type and the holders of that survey number and its sub-divisions. Changes after a sale, inheritance or partition come through mutation, so read the latest record and check for pending applications or court cases where a decision depends on it. Always match the full sub-division number, not just the main survey number.

5. Area: acres and guntas

Telangana records give area in acres and guntas. 40 guntas make 1 acre, so one gunta is about 101.17 square metres and one acre is about 0.405 hectare. An entry such as 2.15 usually means 2 acres 15 guntas, not 2.15 acres, which is a common mistake when areas are copied into a spreadsheet. Convert from the recorded value and state the conversion in any land schedule.

6. Government and assigned land

The record also shows whether land is government land, such as land kept for tanks, roads, burial grounds or other public use, and whether it is assigned land given to a landless holder under conditions that limit its transfer. Some land is also listed as prohibited from registration. These classes are written in the record and not on the map, so the map alone cannot tell you which survey numbers are government or assigned land. Read the current record of each survey number and take advice from a revenue professional before relying on it. Our guide on checking government land in a village covers the wider method.

7. Why a viewer map is not yet a GIS map

A map on a screen is good for finding a survey number, but it is not a file you can open with GPS points or a project layout. For that, the map must be georeferenced, meaning fitted to real coordinates such as WGS 84 latitude / longitude or UTM zone 44N metres, and every survey number digitized as a closed polygon carrying its number. The result should then be checked on satellite imagery: field bunds, roads and tank edges seen from above should sit on the survey lines across the whole village. A map placed only from one or two points can sit tens of metres off, which matters for a plant boundary or a line route.

8. Common uses

  • Seeing a survey number on a satellite image in Google Earth
  • Listing the survey numbers inside a proposed solar park, warehouse or industrial site
  • Listing the survey numbers crossed by a road, pipeline or transmission line route
  • Preparing a survey-wise land schedule with village, survey number, extent and land type

9. Limits to keep in mind

The village map shows the survey as drawn, and the ground has often changed since: new sub-divisions, roads, house plots and tank edges. The maps of neighbouring villages do not always agree exactly along their shared edge. A digitized map is a planning base, not proof of title. The revenue record remains the legal source, and where a line decides ownership or compensation, a DGPS or Total Station survey on the ground is the next step.
