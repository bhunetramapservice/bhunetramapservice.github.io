Land record digitization and the map

India's land-record modernisation (DILRMP, started in 2008 as NLRMP) has put most written records and many village maps online on state portals such as Bhunaksha, Bhulekh, Bhoomi or Mahabhunakasha, and a 14-digit ULPIN (Bhu-Aadhaar) is being given to land parcels. What a portal shows is a picture of the map; what a project team needs is the same map as a georeferenced GIS layer joined to the plot numbers. That is the work we do.

Questions

What is cadastral map digitization?

Turning a village cadastral (khasra / survey) map into a digital GIS file: every plot becomes a closed polygon with its number and area, placed in real coordinates so that it sits on the ground in Google Earth, AutoCAD, QGIS or ArcGIS.

How is it different from land record digitization?

Land record digitization usually means the written records - jamabandi, khatauni, 7/12, RTC, patta - put into a database. Cadastral map digitization is the map side: where each plot lies. Projects need both joined by the plot number, and we deliver the map with a plot list in Excel.

What is a georeferenced cadastral map?

A cadastral map moved, turned and scaled onto its true position on the earth (WGS 84 / UTM). We check every village against satellite imagery so that the plot lines sit on the real field edges.

Which formats do you deliver?

KML / KMZ (Google Earth), DWG / DXF (AutoCAD), SHP (QGIS / ArcGIS) and Excel; PDF map sheets on request.
